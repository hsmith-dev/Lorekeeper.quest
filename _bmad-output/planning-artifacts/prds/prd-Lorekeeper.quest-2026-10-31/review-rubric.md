# PRD Quality Review — Lorekeeper living campaign world

## Overall verdict
This is a strong, well-grounded brownfield PRD. It has a clear thesis ("writing the session journal is how the world gets updated"), honest tensions (the 8 GB baseline is already exceeded, the AGPL/GPL wording, espeak-ng), counter-metrics, a hard spoiler gate, and existing-code references that check out against the repo. The risk is in the privacy model and the launch boundary. Visibility rules cover only some record types, and the unit of a "fact" (for Secrets and NPC grants and hides) is undefined, so architecture and EV-4 inherit gaps on the PRD's single hard gate. No launch cut line is defined, which leaves a 55-FR scope with no fallback if the capstone runs short. A few FR bounds, most importantly caption lag, need tightening before stories are written.

## Decision-readiness — adequate
Decisions are stated as decisions: FR-9 is called "P0" and "should ship regardless of the rest of the expansion". Visibility defaults are decided in FR-8 rather than "considered". Rejected options are named with reasons in addendum §A (for example "Piper (now GPL-3.0)" and "react-leaflet (Hippocratic-2.1…)"). The four owner questions in §12 are genuinely open, each with a proposed default and the cost of the alternative. Q2, for example, says "Player is privacy-first but may surprise current collaborators". The single `[NOTE FOR PM]` sits on a real tension: NFR-2 says "the baseline is already over budget".

The gap is the launch boundary. §9 is "Proposed release phases (not locked)" and runs through Phase 2 with all ten features. The PRD never says what "launch" means for a launch-grade capstone, whether Phase 2 must complete, or which features drop first if spikes or schedule go badly. A second, quieter interaction is that the proposed Q2 default changes what FR-9 actually fixes.

### Findings
- **[high]** No launch cut line or de-scope order (§9, §0, frontmatter stakes) — §9 sequences by dependency, but nothing says which phase or DS set is the launch and capstone bar, and nothing ranks what goes if time runs out. F9 stats, F10 maps and F4 Live Session are scheduled alongside the thesis features with no relative priority. This is the decision a capstone owner most needs and the one epics/sprint planning cannot infer. *Fix:* Add a "Launch definition" line: the phases or DS-1 to DS-5 that must pass to launch. Add an ordered de-scope list, for example F9 → F10 → FR-30 → F4, with what each loss costs the demo.
- **[medium]** FR-9's chat-leak fix is largely moot under the proposed Q2 default (FR-9, FR-7, §12 Q2) — FR-9 frames chat retrieval exposing GM content "for every Member" as a P0 leak. FR-7 proposes migrating every existing Member to Co-GM, which sees everything by definition. On the proposed defaults, the only P0 change that ships for existing users is the read-only Share Link narrowing. The chat fix only protects Players who join later. The PRD doesn't say this, so the owner can't see that choosing Q2's default effectively keeps the chat behaviour for current collaborators. *Fix:* State the interaction in Q2, for example "under Co-GM migration, existing Members keep full chat visibility; FR-9's chat fix applies to new Players only".

## Substance over theater — strong
Little furniture. There are three JTBD personas (GM, Player, self-hoster/reviewer), and each drives decisions: the GM drives the Review Checklist, the Player drives Visibility and the portal, and the reviewer drives §7 evals and NFR-1. NFRs carry product-specific thresholds: NFR-6 is a per-operation table, NFR-3 says "No third resident LLM is added", NFR-4 names the licence classes that fail CI. The Vision is specific to this product: "a continuity checker that catches the dead NPC who walks back in". There is no innovation section padding the document. NFR-10 is the one thin spot (see Done-ness).

## Strategic coherence — strong
The thesis is explicit in §1 and the structure follows it. F1 is labelled "foundation" and §5.1 states "F2, F3, F6 and F7 depend on this feature". The primary SMs (SM-1 to SM-4) measure the quality of the thesis mechanisms (extraction, continuity, knowledge boundary, spoiler safety), not activity. Counter-metrics are present and well-chosen: SM-C1 "flooding the Review Checklist burdens the GM", SM-C2 over-refusal, SM-C3 "False alarms take priority over detection gains". The MVP kind is legible as problem-solving plus a capstone showcase, and the "keepsakes" (F5, F8) are honestly framed as riding along ("The keepsakes come with it").

### Findings
- **[medium]** SM-6 is self-scored and duplicates SM-1 (§7 SM-6) — "At least 70% of proposals approved… across the demo run". The demo GM is the owner approving a scripted run whose proposals EV-1 already hand-labels, so acceptance is just EV-1 precision with a human in the loop. It is the closest thing to a thesis metric ("stop being the group's database") yet measures nothing independent. *Fix:* Either derive SM-6 explicitly from EV-1 labels and drop the "approved" framing, or replace it with a GM-burden measure such as review time per entry or edits per approved item on the demo run, paired with SM-C1.

## Done-ness clarity — adequate
Most FRs have crisp, testable consequences. FR-1 has "returns a Job ID in under 1 second" and "retries at most twice". FR-46 has a concrete Unicode string. FR-15 says "the same proposal is not raised again for that entry". FR-25 bounds information leakage. Several consequences are still adjectives or missing, and one numeric bound is internally wrong.

### Findings
- **[high]** Caption-lag bound does not prevent unbounded backlog (NFR-6 "Live caption lag ≤ 30 s"; FR-26; R-2) — With 10 to 30 s chunks, a 30 s chunk-end-to-text lag on 10 s chunks means a real-time factor of 3, and the backlog grows without limit, which is exactly the failure R-2 describes. Passing NFR-6 can therefore coexist with FR-26 hitting its 10-minute backlog skip in every session. *Fix:* Bound the sustained real-time factor (for example "processing time per chunk ≤ chunk duration, sustained over a 3-hour session with the LLM busy") in addition to per-chunk lag. Tie the chunk size to SPIKE-2's output.
- **[high]** Visibility rules cover only some record types (FR-8, FR-11, FR-12, FR-16, FR-50) — FR-8 enumerates "Journal Entry, Entity, Relationship, Canon Note, source document, Map and Pin". It gives no rule for Mentions (FR-12 highlights Entities in a Party Narrative, which could reveal a GM-only Entity's existence), Entity change history (FR-16's history tab cites source entries, so a Player on a Party Entity may see citations to GM-only entries), NPC Conversation logs (who can read a Player's conversation; FR-38), Live Session transcripts and Rolling Recaps, Job status rows (Job labels can name Entities), and Milestones ("100 Entities in the world" counts GM-only Entities unless filtered). EV-4 enumerates routes, but it can only test rules that exist. *Fix:* Extend FR-8 with a per-type rule table covering every stored record type, including derived and inherited ones. For example: Mention visibility = the stricter of its entry and its Entity; history rows inherit their source entry's Visibility. Seed EV-4 canaries for each.
- **[medium]** FRs with no or token consequences (FR-34, FR-55, FR-44) — FR-34 ("Players hear Party recaps") and FR-55 ("Pins in the World Model") have no consequences block. FR-44 has one, which covers only role change and not invite or removal. A removed Member losing access, including open SSE streams and live Share Links, is the testable edge. *Fix:* Add consequences, for example "a GM-only Narrated Recap is absent from the Player Portal response (EV-4)", "the proposal only appears when the Location is new and at least one Map exists", "a removed Member's next request returns 403, including in-flight streams".
- **[medium]** Adjective bounds where numbers are needed (FR-24 "without visible stutter" on "a mid-range laptop browser"; FR-52 "pan and zoom smoothly"; FR-40 "Works on a phone-sized screen") — none of these can be verified by a story's acceptance test. *Fix:* Name the hardware or browser and a frame-rate or interaction-latency floor, for example "≥ 30 fps during pan on [reference laptop], Chrome current". For FR-40, list the portal views that must be usable at 375 px without horizontal scroll.
- **[low]** FR-27 "covers the whole session… no silent truncation" has no test method — *Fix:* Specify a check, for example "a fact stated only in minute 5 of a 2-hour recorded clip appears in the final Rolling Recap".
- **[low]** FR-15 "Approve all… does not apply items the GM has edited and not yet confirmed" conflicts with "Editing an item changes its proposed values before approval", which implies edit and approve are separate steps. It is unclear whether an "edited, unconfirmed" state exists. *Fix:* Define the item states (pending, edited, approved, rejected) and what "Approve all" does to each.
- **[low]** NFR-10 accessibility has no bound ("accessible list view") — *Fix:* Name a target, for example WCAG 2.2 AA for new pages, keyboard-operable Review Checklist.

## Scope honesty — adequate
Non-goals (§10) do real work: "AI that writes to Canon without GM approval, in any feature" and "Real-time collaborative co-editing of entries". Assumptions are tagged inline and indexed. Delegated decisions are proposed openly in §11. The open-items density is roughly 18 assumption tags, 4 owner questions and 1 PM note. That is high for a green-light PRD, but most items are provisional numbers explicitly gated on Phase 0 spikes, and Q4 is correctly called out as blocking Phase 1. Three omissions are silent rather than declared.

### Findings
- **[medium]** Delete and Visibility-change cascades are undefined (FR-6, FR-8, FR-16) — Journal Entries can already be deleted ("Today entries can only be created and deleted"). The PRD never says what happens to Canon, Mentions, Relationships, NPC Knowledge and citations derived from a deleted entry. It also doesn't say what happens when an entry flips Party → GM-only after its Party-visible derived records were approved. The second case is a spoiler path. *Fix:* Add consequences to FR-6 and FR-8. For example: deleting an entry marks derived Mentions as removed and flags Canon fields whose only source was that entry for GM review; tightening an entry's Visibility prompts the GM to tighten derived records.
- **[medium]** Hosted and community-server deployments aren't addressed (§2.2, §10, NFR-8, addendum §A FR-1) — The repo README supports "free community servers" and paid hosted services under the commercial licence, with Stripe dormant in code. NFR-8 keeps "hosted-usage metering". The PRD frames everything around one group's Reference Box, so it is silent on how STT minutes, TTS and NLI Jobs are metered for hosted users, and on whether "concurrency 1 per heavy model" lets one campaign's backfill starve every other campaign on a shared server. *Fix:* Either add a non-goal ("multi-campaign servers get no fairness or metering guarantees in v1") or add an NFR covering per-campaign Job fairness and which new AI operations call `record_hosted_usage`.
- **[low]** Phase 0 FRs depend on later-phase FRs (FR-6 consequences reference FR-18 and FR-19; §9 puts FR-6 in Phase 0 and FR-18 and FR-19 in Phases 1 and 2) — *Fix:* Phrase FR-6's re-extraction and continuity consequences as "when F1/F2 are present".
- **[low]** Stale pending proposals after an edit (FR-6, FR-18) — An edit queues re-extraction, but the fate of pending proposals derived from the old text is unstated (superseded, kept, merged). *Fix:* State that an edit supersedes that entry's pending, unedited proposals.

## Downstream usability — adequate
The Glossary is thorough and explicitly binding ("Downstream documents must use these terms exactly"). IDs are contiguous (FR-1 to FR-55, NFR-1 to NFR-10, UJ-1 to UJ-6, SM-1 to SM-7 plus SM-C1 to SM-C4, EV-1 to EV-5, DS-1 to DS-5, R-1 to R-8), and cross-references resolve. Each feature names the UJ it realizes. All UJs have a named protagonist (Dana, Sam). The addendum gives architecture concrete extension points without leaking mechanism into FRs. One core noun is not actually defined.

### Findings
- **[high]** "Fact" is used as a unit of permission but never defined (Glossary "Secret… a GM-only field or fact"; "NPC Knowledge. The set of facts"; FR-36 "grant or hide individual Journal Entries or facts"; SM-3 "0 on GM-hidden facts"; EV-3 "one GM-hidden fact") — It is unclear whether a fact is an Entity field, a Mention quote, a sentence span within a Narrative or a Canon Note. Addendum §A's `npc_knowledge(entity, entry, how)` supports whole entries only, so architecture will either drop fact-level grants or invent a model. The same ambiguity decides whether a GM can hide one spoiler sentence inside a Party Narrative, which is UJ-3's traitor case. *Fix:* Add **Fact** to the Glossary with its storage unit, or restrict grants, hides and Secrets to named units (Journal Entry, Entity field, Canon Note) and remove "fact" elsewhere.
- **[low]** "Chronicler" (UJ-3, §5.7, FR-43) is not in the Glossary and collides with **Chronicle** (the book export). *Fix:* Use "grounded chat" consistently, or add "Chronicler: the grounded chat assistant" to the Glossary.

## Shape fit — strong
The shape matches a chain-top brownfield product with meaningful multi-role UX. UJs are load-bearing because Player versus GM visibility is the product's hard constraint. Foundations precede features because the recon found missing capabilities. Existing-code references were spot-checked against the repo and are accurate:
- `src/backend/app/api/routes/share.py` exposes `/{token}/journals`, `/{token}/journals/{id}`, `/{token}/npcs` and `/{token}/quests`.
- Migration head `a3e7c1f9d258` exists and nothing revises it.
- `.github/workflows/backend-tests.yml` triggers on `branches: [master]`.
- Production runs `gunicorn -w 4`, and the ~6.4 GB idle comment matches.
- LICENSE is AGPL-3.0.
- `NarrativeReviewPanel`, `NpcDetailPanel` and `RecordSessionPage` exist.

Existing versus new behaviour is consistently distinguished ("replacing today's name-substring lookup", "Today entries can only be created and deleted").

### Findings
- **[medium]** espeak-ng may be the primary phonemizer, not a "fallback", in the recommended runtime (NFR-4, R-6, §11, addendum §A FR-31 to FR-34 and §F) — The PRD and addendum describe espeak-ng as Kokoro's "fallback". That holds for the reference `misaki` G2P, where espeak handles out-of-vocabulary words. The recommended `kokoro-onnx` package is reported to phonemize through espeak-ng directly (this needs verifying). If so, "fallback off in commercial builds" would disable TTS entirely, and Q1's proposed answer doesn't work. *Fix:* Have LIC-1 explicitly confirm which runtime (kokoro-onnx, Kokoro-FastAPI or misaki) works with espeak-ng absent. Make §11 and Q1 conditional on the result.

## Mechanical notes
- Assumptions Index roundtrip is clean: every inline `[ASSUMPTION]` (§2.3, FR-3, FR-5, FR-7 ×2, FR-8, FR-14, FR-24, FR-26, FR-29, FR-33, FR-39, FR-42, FR-51 ×2, FR-52, §7, §11 ×2) appears in §13. The §13 entry "NFR-6: All latency targets provisional" has no inline `[ASSUMPTION]` tag in NFR-6, though it is stated as provisional in prose. This is acceptable, but add the tag for symmetry.
- NFR-4 uses a non-standard `[NOTE: …]` callout. Use `[NOTE FOR PM: …]` so tooling and readers pick it up alongside NFR-2's.
- Glossary case drift: "Quest progress" (Review Checklist group label, FR-15, UJ-1) versus "Quest Progress" (the defined term). "Party-visible" is used throughout, but the Glossary defines only "Party". Both are minor; consider noting "Party-visible = Visibility Party" in the Glossary.
- "Co-GM" is defined inside the Role entry but used as if it were a Role ("migrated to Co-GM", FR-44 "from Co-GM to Player"). Clarify whether Co-GM is a distinct stored Role value or a GM who is not the Owner, because it affects the `campaign_members.role` enum in addendum §B.
- The internal memlog records "3 genre narrator presets", while the PRD (FR-33, §11) says about five, one per genre. The PRD is authoritative; the memlog is stale.
- All required sections for launch-grade brownfield stakes are present (Vision, users, UJs, Glossary, FRs, NFRs, metrics with counter-metrics, risks, phases, non-goals, open questions, assumptions index).
